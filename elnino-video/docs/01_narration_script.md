These pieces reinforce one another, in what's called the Bjerknes feedback, after the meteorologist Jacob Bjerknes. **[PAUSE]** Strong trade winds drive upwelling, which keeps the east cold. ## Scene 8 (s08) — Decay and La Niña

El Niño doesn't last forever. While Kelvin waves carry warm water east, slower ocean waves, called Rossby waves, travel west. They bounce off the western edge of the Pacific and return east, gradually lifting the thermocline. Meanwhile, El Niño drains heat away from the equator. **[PAUSE]** The trade winds recover, upwelling returns, and the east cools. Sometimes the system overshoots into La Niña, El Niño's counterpart: stronger trade winds, an unusually cold eastern Pacific, and weather that is often roughly the reverse of El Niño's.

Together, El Niño and La Niña form ENSO, the El Niño–Southern Oscillation.
